# Wizard-edge wager-clipping summary

This report measures the effect of limiting cumulative gross wagers in a blackjack round. It uses the learned Full-deviation Hi-Lo policies and the rule/deck-specific Wizard edge.

## Methodology

- Kelly multiplier: `@1`
- Samples: 1,000 Kelly measurements × 1,000,000 rounds = 1 billion rounds per result
- Payouts: 3:2 and 6:5
- Decks: 1, 2, and 6
- Maximum total-wager fractions: 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00
- The 1.00 result is the unclipped reference.
- Exposure-table cells are `P(total gross wager / bankroll) / P(|fX|)`.
- Except where an explicit 0% row is shown, the 0–1% exposure bin includes no-wager rounds. The wager-multiple tables separate no-wager rounds as multiple 0.
- Duplicate 0.75 artifacts were de-duplicated using the payout, deck count, and cap stored inside `results.json`.

## Main conclusions

- A 10% cap materially reduces Kelly growth in the one- and two-deck cases.
- A 25% cap still has a measurable effect in the one-deck cases.
- A 50% cap retains nearly all of the unclipped growth.
- A 75% cap is effectively indistinguishable from the 1.00 reference at this simulation resolution.
- Lower caps change playing opportunities as well as bet size: when the cumulative cap prevents another wager, splitting or doubling is unavailable.

## Important cap-enforcement anomaly

The nominal 10% cap was exceeded in rare near-zero-bankroll rounds because the affordability comparison currently includes an absolute floating-point tolerance:

- 3:2, 1 deck: 8,782 rounds above 10.1% (0.0008782% of rounds), with a maximum total wager of 50%.
- 6:5, 1 deck: 97 rounds above 10.1% (0.0000097% of rounds), with a maximum total wager of 15.463%.

The aggregate probabilities and growth estimates are barely affected, but these maxima must not be interpreted as strict cap guarantees until the tolerance is corrected.
